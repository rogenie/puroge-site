# Keyword map: what people are already searching

Researched November 2026. There's no paid keyword tool connected yet, so volumes come from public reports
and current search results. Treat them as direction, not exact counts. Re-check with Google Search Console
once the site has been live for about a month.

## What the data says

- **Linen demand is large and rising.** One report estimates "linen clothing womens" at ~14,800 searches/month
  and "linen vs cotton" at ~5,400 (GEOly, Linen Clothing AI Visibility Report 2026). Rolling Stone (Jul 2026)
  reports search interest in linen pants was more than triple 2025's level.
- **It's seasonal.** "Linen pants" and "linen shirt" peak May–August and are lowest in December–January
  (Accio, Google Trends analysis). Publishing in November means pages can be indexed and earning trust
  before the spring peak.
- **"Sustainable clothing" is the main term in its category.** It gets more search interest than "sustainable accessories" or
  "sustainable footwear" (Accio, Feb 2025–Jan 2026). U.S. shoppers say "sustainable" far more than "eco-friendly"
  (SourceReady, US Sustainable Fashion Market Report 2026). Use "sustainable", not "eco-friendly".
- **People ask about quality, repair and cost per wear.** Searches for repair, longevity and "quality over quantity"
  are rising (Benedetti, 2026). This is the upper-middle "buy once" shopper.
- **Big questions people ask about linen:** is it appropriate for work, is it good for travel, how to stop wrinkles, is it see-through.
  Brands like Quince, Luxmii, Coofandy and Fibflx already answer these with brand-voice FAQ pages. They can be
  beaten with better sources and a stronger argument.
- **Health questions bring people in.** "Is polyester bad for you" is covered by NBC Select, Sustainable
  Jungle and others. It leads straight into Purogé's "why humans should wear natural fiber" story.

## Rules for every new article

- One primary keyword. Put it in the title, first answer and URL slug.
- Answer it in the first 40–60 words (the "short answer" box).
- Phrase headings as questions where it reads naturally.
- Every statistic gets a footnote to its original source. No unsourced numbers.
- Link to the pillar and to at least two related articles.
