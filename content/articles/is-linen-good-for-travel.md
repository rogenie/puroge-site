## Read this if you’ve ever landed feeling like you slept in plastic

Twelve hours in a cabin. A connection. A taxi straight to dinner. The “performance” travel pants you bought for exactly this trip have been holding heat against you since takeoff.

Linen is the fabric people have chosen for heat and long days for a very long time. Here is why it travels so well, and how to deal with the one thing everyone worries about.

## Why linen works on the road

- **It breathes and dries fast.** Flax is faster drying than cotton,[^cfda] so it handles a warm cabin, a humid city and an overnight hand wash.
- **It takes a beating.** Linen is one of the strongest natural fibers, so it stands up to suitcases and hotel laundry.[^moss]
- **It dresses up and down.** The same linen top works with sandals at the market and with leather shoes at dinner, which means fewer pieces in the bag.[^moss]
- **It looks better lived-in.** Linen is meant to look relaxed. You don’t need to arrive pressed.[^moss]

## The one real problem: creases

Linen wrinkles. There’s no honest way around it. But the creases are soft, they relax as you wear it, and most of them can be avoided before you leave home.

> Synthetic travel clothes promise no wrinkles. Linen promises you’ll be comfortable. Only one of those matters on hour eleven.

## How to pack linen

1. **Pack it completely dry.** Even slightly damp linen creases badly and can turn musty in a hot destination.[^sondeflor]
2. **Roll, don’t fold.** Loose rolls avoid hard fold lines.
3. **Pack it on top.** Keep heavy items underneath.
4. **Hang it on arrival.** Then let the shower steam do the work.
5. **Carry a small mist bottle.** A light spray of water and a smooth by hand fixes most creases in minutes.

## The two-piece travel formula

<div class="rule" markdown="1">
<p class="label">Pack this first</p>

One linen top and one pair of comfortable pants in neutral colors. Build every other outfit around them.
</div>

Wear the pants on the plane. Pair the top with them for day one, with a skirt or jeans for day two, and with a jacket for dinner. Add one knit for the cabin and one pair of good leather shoes. That’s a week.

## What to do next

Before your next trip, lay out what you normally pack and check the labels. Replace the synthetic “travel” pieces you wear most with natural fiber, starting with the pants you fly in.

<p class="ps"><strong>P.S.</strong> Choose linen in a mid-tone or darker color for travel. Navy, olive and tobacco hide creases and the odd coffee spill far better than white.</p>
