## Read this if you're afraid to put your linen in the machine

You paid for good linen. Then you stood over the washing machine and hesitated.

Hot or cold? Delicates or normal? Will it come out a size smaller, or stiff as a board?

So it sits in the hamper. Or it goes to the dry cleaner, at a cost, every time.

Here is the short version: linen is made to be washed. You only need to stop washing it like a towel.

## What actually wears linen out?

Flax is a strong fiber. The CFDA describes it as strong and durable, and stronger and stiffer than cotton. It also notes that flax does not pill.[^cfda]

So linen rarely fails from being worn. It fails from heat, twisting and harsh chemistry.

The Alliance for European Flax-Linen and Hemp is direct about it: too much spin can wrinkle flax and reduce its longevity.[^alliance] Excessive dryer heat can alter the fabric and possibly cause shrinkage.[^alliance] Bleaching agents in detergent are best avoided.[^alliance]

In other words, the habits that damage linen are the default settings on most machines.

## How much does washing really matter?

More than the field it grew in.

The CFDA cites a 2008 report from the European linen confederation (CELC) finding that almost 80% of linen's energy and water consumption comes from washing and ironing the garment.[^cfda] Cultivation, it notes, is the least water and energy intensive part of the life cycle.[^cfda]

The same pattern holds across a wardrobe. WRAP's UK study *Valuing our clothes* estimates that laundry accounts for around one-quarter of the carbon footprint of clothing.[^wrap]

And heat is most of the cost. ENERGY STAR says water heating consumes about 90% of the energy it takes to run a clothes washer.[^es] When Which? tested six machines, washing at 30°C instead of 40°C used 38% less energy, and washing at 20°C cut energy use by 62%.[^which]

> The cooler wash is the kinder wash: for the fiber, the color and the bill.

## It isn't your fault

Most of us learned laundry on cotton towels and polyester gym kit. Hot, full, fast spin, tumble dry, done.

Care labels don't explain why. They print a symbol and leave you to guess what happens if you ignore it.

And linen's reputation doesn't help. "Delicate" sounds fragile, so people either baby it with dry cleaning or give up and treat it like everything else. Neither is necessary.

## The new way: the cool-and-slow rule

<div class="rule" markdown="1">
<p class="label">The cool-and-slow rule</p>

Wash linen cool, spin it slow, dry it gently. Heat and force are the only real enemies.
</div>

Everything below is a version of that sentence.

## How to wash linen, step by step

**Read the label first.** Linen is often blended or lined, so check the care label before anything else.[^ariel] If it says dry clean, it usually means the construction (linings, buttons) needs it.[^alliance]

**Sort by color.** Wash linen with similar colors, or on its own. Cold water helps protect colors from dye transfer.[^ariel]

**Choose a gentle cycle at 30°C or cooler.** The Alliance recommends a delicate program at 30°C for shirts and trousers.[^alliance] The CFDA's advice to brands is the same message for owners: wash less, in cold water, and hang dry.[^cfda]

**Use a mild detergent without bleach.** The Alliance suggests a mild, preferably natural detergent with no bleaching agents.[^alliance] Liquid dissolves easily in cool water.

**Don't overload the drum.** Linen needs room to move, and an overstuffed drum makes hard creases.[^parachute]

**Spin low, or not at all.** A low spin is advised, and no spin is better where possible.[^alliance]

**Take it out straight away. Don't wring.** Shake it once and reshape it by hand. Heavy wringing leaves marked folds.[^alliance]

<div class="callout" markdown="1">
<p class="label">Why cooler and shorter helps color</p>

A University of Leeds and Procter & Gamble study, reported by Tom's Guide, compared wash cycles on activewear t-shirts. A 30-minute cycle at 25°C reduced dye transfer by up to 74% and almost halved microfiber loss compared with an 85-minute cycle at 40°C.[^toms] The garments weren't linen, but the principle carries: heat and time pull dye out of fabric.
</div>

## How to dry linen without shrinking it

**Line dry in the shade.** Dry linen in the open air, away from direct sunlight.[^alliance] ENERGY STAR notes that air-drying saves energy and also helps clothes last longer.[^es]

**If you tumble, go low.** Use a low temperature and gentle program, and remove the piece while it's still slightly damp.[^alliance] Over-drying can leave linen stiff.[^ariel]

**Expect the first wash to tell you the most.** Linen's biggest shrink comes in the first wash, mostly from hot water and hot drying. Prewashed linen has already done it.[^parachute]

## How to iron and store linen

**Iron while slightly damp,** on the reverse side, at a medium to high setting, usually around 200°C. If it's dry, use steam.[^alliance] (For the full routine on creases, see our guide to keeping linen from wrinkling.)

**Hang shirts and tops** once ironed.[^alliance]

**Store it cool and dry.** Ariel advises keeping linen in a cool, dry place and avoiding plastic bags, cardboard boxes and cedar chests.[^ariel] Linen needs to breathe in the closet too.

## Why care is the most sustainable thing you'll do

WRAP estimated that extending the average life of clothes by just three months of active use per item would cut each of their carbon, water and waste footprints by 5 to 10%.[^wrap] Cutting laundry frequency and temperature, filling loads properly and skipping summer tumble drying could reduce the footprint by a further 7%.[^wrap]

So the most sustainable linen is the piece you already own, washed a little less, a little cooler, and kept for longer.

## What to keep in your rotation

If you're building around a few pieces you'll wear often, make it a linen top and a pair of linen pants you're not afraid to wash. Cool water, slow spin, a hanger in the shade. That's the whole care routine, and it fits into an ordinary Sunday.

## Who this isn't for

If a garment is structured, lined or labeled dry clean only, this routine isn't for it. Follow the label, or a good cleaner.

And if you want linen that stays crisp and new-looking forever, gentle washing won't give you that. Linen softens with every wash. That's part of the deal, and most people come to prefer it.

## What to do next

Look at your machine's cycle list tonight. Find the gentle or delicate program, set it to 30°C or cooler, and turn the spin down. Save it as your linen wash, if your machine allows it.
