## Read this if you’re standing between two shirts

One says 100% linen. One says 100% cotton. The linen one costs more and you’re not sure why. Both are “natural.” Both are “breathable.”

They are not the same fabric, and the difference matters most for the pieces you wear the longest: the shirt you wear to work, the pants you fly in.

## The side-by-side

| | Linen | Cotton |
|---|---|---|
| Plant | Flax stem | Cotton seed boll |
| Fiber length | Up to 90 cm | Up to about 3.5 cm |
| Strength | Stronger, stiffer | Softer, weaker |
| Drying | Faster | Slower |
| Feel when new | Crisp, textured | Soft |
| Wrinkles | A lot | Some |
| Lifespan with care | Decades | Years |
| Growing inputs | Often rain-fed; less fertilizer and pesticide | Often irrigated; more chemical-intensive |

Fiber length, strength and drying figures are from the CFDA’s fiber guide.[^cfda][^cfda2]

## Why linen wins on the body

**It moves heat and moisture.** Flax is stronger, stiffer and faster drying than cotton.[^cfda] In practice, that means a linen shirt stands slightly away from the skin instead of clinging, and dries before you notice it was damp.

**It lasts.** Fiber length is the quiet secret of quality. Long fibers spin into strong, smooth yarn that resists pilling. Flax fibers are many times longer than cotton’s,[^cfda2] and well-cared-for linen can last up to three decades.[^goy]

> Cotton feels best the day you buy it. Linen feels best five years later.

## Why linen wins on the planet

Flax cultivation needs little irrigation, is often fed by rainfall alone, and requires significantly less fertilizer and pesticide than cotton.[^cfda] Over the lifetime of a garment, one estimate puts linen’s water use at just under a quarter of its cotton counterpart.[^euronews]

The honest caveat: growing is the low-impact part. The CFDA notes that processing and consumer care can carry a much higher impact than cultivation.[^cfda2] Washing cool and line-drying matters as much as the fiber you pick.

## Where cotton is the right call

We wear cotton too. It is the better choice when:

- **You want softness from day one.** T-shirts, sleepwear, anything right against sensitive skin.
- **You can’t abide creases.** Cotton poplin and twill hold a press longer.
- **Price is the deciding factor.** Organic cotton is a good, lower-cost natural fiber.

What we avoid is neither. It’s the polyester “linen-look” shirt that gives you the creases without the breathability.

## The rule for choosing

<div class="rule" markdown="1">
<p class="label">Choose by how often you’ll wear it</p>

Wear it often, in heat, at work or in transit? Linen. Wear it for softness and ease at home? Cotton.
</div>

## What to do next

Start with the piece you wear most when it’s warm or when you’re traveling. For most people, that is a top and a pair of pants.

<p class="ps"><strong>P.S.</strong> If you’re unsure, pick the linen in a darker or textured weave. It shows fewer creases and you’ll learn quickly why people who try linen rarely go back.</p>
